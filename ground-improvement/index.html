Ground improvement in Columbia South Carolina

Technical studies that support your project.

LEARN MORE

Ground improvement in Columbia, South Carolina addresses the challenges of building on the region’s variable residual soils and soft alluvial deposits common to the Piedmont and Coastal Plain transition. Techniques must comply with IBC Chapter 18 and SCDOT specifications, often requiring deep foundation alternatives when bearing capacity falls below 2,000 psf. Our stone column design reinforces compressible clays, while vibrocompaction design densifies loose granular fills to mitigate settlement risk in Midlands geology.

These solutions support warehouse slabs, bridge approaches, and mid-rise structures where uncontrolled fill or high groundwater demands engineered stabilization. Integrating stone columns with preloading or rigid inclusions often delivers the necessary performance for heavily loaded footings. We target liquefaction mitigation and total settlement control to keep Columbia projects on schedule and within bearing criteria.

Common questions

What type of corrosion protection do anchors need in Columbia?

Class II double encapsulation is standard for permanent anchors in the Piedmont residual soils. The saprolite here is acidic, with a pH often below 5.0, so a corrugated HDPE duct with grout inside and outside the sheath is required to meet the 75-year design life in PTI DC35.1.

How deep do anchors need to go to reach competent rock?

In much of downtown Columbia and the St. Andrews area, competent bedrock can be found between 15 and 25 feet. However, we recommend a pre-production test pit program because the depth to refusal can vary by 10 feet over a distance of 50 feet.

What is the cost range for an anchor design package?

A full design package, including submittal drawings, calculations, and on-site proof testing oversight, typically ranges from US$960 to US$4,170 depending on the number of anchors and the complexity of the load cases.

Do you perform the proof testing or just design the anchors?

Our team oversees the initial lift-off test and the 60-minute creep test on the sacrificial anchor. We then review the contractor's proof test data for every production anchor to confirm the apparent bond strength matches the design assumptions.

Can a passive anchor replace a retaining wall on a steep lot near Lake Murray?

It depends on the slope geometry and the depth of the soil. A row of passive dowels can reinforce a near-vertical cut in rock, but if the saprolite is thicker than 4 feet, you will likely need a facing system with a retaining wall and active tiebacks to prevent raveling.
